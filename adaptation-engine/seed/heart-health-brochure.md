# Understanding Your Heart Risk

Northern Metropolitan Health Service

## What is cardiovascular risk?

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) affects the heart and blood vessels. Your absolute
cardiovascular risk is the probability of experiencing a cardiovascular event,
such as a heart attack or stroke, within the next five years. It is calculated
using a combination of factors including your age, sex, blood pressure,
cholesterol levels, smoking status and whether you have diabetes.

If your calculated risk is 15% or higher, you are considered to be at high risk.
Approximately 1 in 4 Australian adults falls into the moderate or high risk
category.

## What raises your risk?

Unfortunately, some risk factors cannot be modified, such as your age, your sex
and your family history. It is only a matter of time before these begin to
affect you.

Other factors are within your control. Smoking doubles your risk of a heart
attack. Quitting smoking halves your risk within one year. Being physically
inactive, carrying excess weight and having a poor diet all contribute.

## What can you do?

- Keep an eye on your blood pressure. Your target should be less than 140/90 mmHg.
- Choose wholegrain bread and have a bowl of porridge for breakfast.
- Aim to be more active.
- Cut down on alcohol.
- Take it easy over the Christmas period, when it is easy to overindulge.

Patients are advised to attend an annual review with their general
practitioner.

## Medicines

If you have been prescribed a statin, take one 20 mg tablet each evening. Do not
stop taking this medicine without speaking to your doctor, even if you feel
well. Stop taking this medicine and contact your doctor if you develop
unexplained muscle pain.

Research shows that statins are effective at reducing cardiovascular events.

## When to seek help

Call an ambulance immediately if you experience chest pain lasting more than 10
minutes, pain spreading to your arm or jaw, shortness of breath, or nausea and
sweating.
