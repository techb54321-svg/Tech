Your data, plainly.

What Ironclad stores, where it lives, and who can see it. No legalese.

✓ No account, ever
✓ No ads, analytics or tracking
✓ Your location never leaves your phone
✓ You can delete everything

Piece by piece

📍Your location

Read on your phoneNever stored or sent

When you check in or out, your phone works out how far you are from your gym's pin. Only that distance is kept, for example "40 m from the pin". Your actual position is never saved and never leaves your phone.

🏋️Your gym's location

On your phone

The pin you set for your gym stays on your phone. To show it on a map, the map tiles load from OpenStreetMap. When you search for a gym by name, the search words go to OpenStreetMap along with a rough area (about 10 km) so nearby gyms come first. Your referee never gets the coordinates; if you move the pin, they're only told it moved and by how much.

📱Phone numbers and email

On your phoneNever sent to a server

Ironclad never asks for your phone number. Your referee's phone or email stays on your phone and is only used to start a message to them when you send a report yourself.

📸Check-in photos

On your phoneAlerts server, 14 days

Photos are optional. They're kept on your phone. With Ironclad alerts on, a copy goes to the alerts server so your referee can see it, and it deletes itself after 14 days.

📊Sessions, verdicts and money

On your phoneAlerts server, if alerts are on

Times you trained, which sessions counted, weekly results, the deposit and what you owe. Your referee sees these, because that's their job.

Who can see what

🤝Your referee

Through their private link they see your name, gym name, schedule, the money, your activity and check-in photos. Never your location or contact details.

The link works like a key. If it gets forwarded, open Settings → Your data → Replace the referee link: the old link and every phone that turned on alerts through it stop working at once.

🛰️The alerts server

Ironclad alerts run on one small server operated by the person who shared Ironclad with you, on Cloudflare. When you turn alerts on, your app gets its own private space there, locked with a secret key only your phone holds. It keeps your first name, your referee's first name, gym name, schedule, the money, recent activity and photos (14 days). Never your location or anyone's phone number or email.

Cloudflare encrypts the storage. Other users can't see your space, and neither can their referees. The person running the server could technically look inside it, the way any app's operator can, so only use alerts if you're comfortable with that. Manual mode sends nothing anywhere.

🔔Notifications

Alerts are encrypted end to end to your referee's phone. Apple and Google deliver them but can't read them.

🌐Everyone else

The app, its fonts and its files come from the same web host (GitHub Pages). Like any website, the host sees your IP address when the app loads. Ironclad loads nothing from Google, Facebook or ad networks.

Backups and deleting

🔐Backups

When you export a backup, Ironclad encrypts it with a passphrase you choose (AES-256). Without the passphrase the file is unreadable, so it's safe in cloud storage. Lose the passphrase and the backup is gone too.

🗑️Deleting everything

  • On your phone: Settings → Danger zone → Wipe everything.
  • On the alerts server: wiping the app deletes your space there too, along with your referee's link. Photos already stored expire within 14 days and can't be opened once the link is gone.
  • Your referee: they can turn alerts off on their page; replacing the link cuts them off entirely.

Honest limits

  • The log shows tampering; it isn't unbreakable. Editing it outside the app is detected and your referee is told, but a determined, technical person could rebuild it.
  • GPS can be faked on a phone that has been modified to do so. Ironclad trusts what your phone reports.
  • Your phone's own security matters. Anyone who can unlock your phone can open the app. Use a passcode.
  • Ironclad currently shares its web address with a few other projects by the same developer. Moving it to its own domain is planned.

Questions about your data? Ask the person who shared Ironclad with you.
